What NBA players and coaches have said about Martin Luther King

13 quotes about Martin Luther King from 12 players, coaches and executives, newest first. Each one links to its original source.

Best quotes

Kenny Smith:

“Bill Russell was my first coach. Bill Russell, he had a rule, I had to sit next to him on every plane ride and every bus ride as a rookie. He would talk and he'd say stuff like, 'Yeah, Kenny, back in our day, I was with, me and Malcolm X would go over here and we go eat.'I'm like, 'Malcolm X?' He's like, 'Yeah, Malcolm X.' He would like, 'Yeah, Martin Luther King Jr.' He would tell me stories about them like they were so casual. The people who made me, like him.”
KSKenny Smith on Martin Luther King, Bill RussellMay 27, 2026YouTube

Gina Mizell:Monty on playing in Memphis on MLK Day:

“What a blessing and an honor... I just hope that it doesn’t just become something we do. I hope it continues to become a season in the year where we really think about what Dr. King sacrificed so that a guy like me could be in this position”
Monty Williams on Martin Luther KingJan 18, 2021Twitter @ginamizell

Austin Rivers:

“My parents had to go through much worse dating in the era they did — a white woman dating a black guy when my mom was in college. We had people in the KKK come burn our house down in San Antonio. I don’t like to talk about this. I was 4 years old. I remember what the house looked like. I remember my mom … There’s been a lot of things happen to my family, a lot of things happen to my mom and dad, specifically. It means a lot more than people think it means. I don’t just have Martin Luther King on my leg for no reason.”
Austin Rivers on Martin Luther KingJan 20, 2019Houston Chronicle

Stotts told The Athletic in a phone interview.

“It’s really important for our history. The way I look at it, some of it is very difficult to get a hold of. Some of the things that were done in our history are not things to be proud of. Kind of an ugly truth at times — but also, the way that African Americans have overcome some of the hurdles that they’ve faced in the history of our country. What Martin Luther King and many others were able to do is something that everyone should know about and everyone should admire.”
TSTerry Stotts on Martin Luther KingFeb 15, 2020The Athletic
