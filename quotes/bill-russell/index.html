Richard Jefferson:

“There is no GOAT in basketball because there's multiple levels. Kareem Abdul-Jabbar I think had the greatest career, Bill Russell won the most championships, Michael Jordan is the icon, and I think Kobe Bryant and what he did mentally for the game and really for athletes all around the world. ButI would just say if you were to build a basketball player, you could not build a better basketball player than LeBron James. There's no skill set he doesn't have, there's no physical ability. So for me, if I was building a basketball player, mentally and physically, he would be the person that I would build.”
Richard Jefferson on LeBron James, Kobe Bryant, Michael Jordan, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Bill RussellSep 12, 2026YouTube

Kenny Smith:

“Bill Russell was my first coach. Bill Russell, he had a rule, I had to sit next to him on every plane ride and every bus ride as a rookie. He would talk and he'd say stuff like, 'Yeah, Kenny, back in our day, I was with, me and Malcolm X would go over here and we go eat.'I'm like, 'Malcolm X?' He's like, 'Yeah, Malcolm X.' He would like, 'Yeah, Martin Luther King Jr.' He would tell me stories about them like they were so casual. The people who made me, like him.”
KSKenny Smith on Martin Luther King, Bill RussellMay 27, 2026YouTube

Dominique Wilkins:

“I don’t believe in the GOAT and all that stuff. How can you say a guy is the greatest of all-time when they haven’t played against some of the guys that were the greatest of their era? Like a Wilt Chamberlain, Bill Russell. “But I will say this: Michael Jordan made six Finals and won six championships with the same team. And he won three championships after retiring to play another sport. Nothing like that has ever been done before. MJ did some things that no one will ever do. So if I’m going with the best player to ever play, that’s where my mindset sits. I’m going with Michael. That’s no disrespect to anybody, but I’m looking at what he accomplished in a short period of time.”
Dominique Wilkins on Michael Jordan, Wilt Chamberlain, Bill RussellJun 2, 2025Esports Insider

Bob Cousy:

“In the 1950s, the most racist city we used to visit was St. Louis. [Russell] had to walk through the crowd to come on to the court and occasionally we’d hear the N-word. Across from where we stayed, there was a greasy spoon and we’d walk in there after the game — the black and white guys — and they’d say the black guys couldn’t eat there and we’d say, ‘Hey, this [expletive] shouldn’t be eaten by any human beings’ and we’d all turn and walk out together. Sports creates that kind of unity. Even if one of your teammates is a criminal, you’d probably support him because he’s going to help you reach the ultimate goal.”
Bob Cousy on Bill RussellJul 15, 2020Boston Globe
